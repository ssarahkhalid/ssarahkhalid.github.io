spec · crossover · a conversion engine for product leaders moving into AI safety

you don't need a PhD to work on AI safety. you need a path.

AI safety's biggest talent gap isn't researchers — it's high-agency product leaders. PMs at fast-growing AI companies sit closer to where frontier capability actually ships than almost anyone, and almost no one is deliberately recruiting them. crossover is that recruitment engine: workshops, a hackathon, a cohort, and a toolkit for making the transition real.

01 · thesis

horizontal influence is a scarce safety contribution — not a consolation prize.

the thing PMs do all day — translating between engineering, leadership, and users; turning ambiguity into shippable specs; holding the line on what "done" means — is exactly the muscle safety work is short on. evals need product thinking. governance needs people who've shipped under constraint. labs and safety orgs need operators who've watched real systems fail real people.

crossover exists because the field has the arguments but not the conversion machinery. it was built by a PM with seven years shipping production AI in healthcare — a domain where if the system fails, a real person gets hurt — and it treats your product instincts as the asset, not the thing to apologize for.

02 · why PMs haven't converted already

two blockers, both fixable.

BLK-01 · the credential myth

"safety means a research PhD."

so capable product people quietly count themselves out before anyone has a chance to tell them otherwise. the field's front doors look like they're built for researchers, because mostly they were.

resolutioncrossover shows the concrete, non-PhD paths — evals, safety product work, governance, field-building — with living proof of people who've walked them.

BLK-02 · the unmade pitch

nobody told you that you're needed.

no one has said, plainly, that PM skills are a scarce and wanted contribution in safety. recruitment in this field is accidental — it runs on people who stumble in.

resolutioncrossover is deliberate recruitment: your influence is the contribution, here's the path, here's the room where it starts.

03 · the funnel

one pipeline, three stages.

crossover starts as a series of workshops, talks, and meetups, and builds toward a full product offering for PM-to-safety transitions. but the spine is the same at every scale:

stage 01attract

a hackathon built for generalists

an AI safety hackathon for generalists in canada — in the lineage of london's "breaking barriers" format — designed as the low-pressure front door. no research background assumed; curiosity does the work.

stage 02convert

a high-intensity cohort

evals, the AI-risk landscape and its competing worldviews, and coaching each person down to one concrete, real project. this is where a curious PM becomes a literate one with a thing to build.

workshop series + 1:1 project coaching
stage 03commit

proof-of-work, on a real stage

eval benchmarks, joint projects, and genuine artifacts that make a PM's transition legible to the field — showcased at the AI safety unconference this november.

capstone: AISU 2026 · november 20–22 · montréal + global

04 · the practice

more technical, still a PM.

you are not an engineer and not a designer — you float between those worlds, and your toolkit is what makes the floating useful. the crossover curriculum upgrades that toolkit for the AI era instead of asking you to abandon it:

spec-driven development

run product work as living specs — constitutions, briefs, proposals, unified specs — so your intent survives contact with agents, engineers, and time.

context engineering

how to context well: structuring knowledge bases, prompts, and artifacts so models (and teammates) do their best work with what you give them.

evals as product craft

designing eval suites, red-teaming your own features, and turning "is it safe?" into something measurable, shippable, and owned.

the AI-risk landscape

a working map of the field — the threat models, the worldviews, the orgs, the open problems — and where a product person plugs in.

workflows, shared openly

real crossover-experiment workflows from working PMs: how they study, what they built, what actually moved their transition forward.

finding your project

coaching from your current role to one concrete safety project — the artifact that turns interest into evidence.

06 · definition of done

strict about what counts.

acceptance criteria

  • a verifiable redirect into high-impact work — not responsible-AI theater, not in-place advocacy with a new job title.
  • the metric is real transitions. not attendance. not vibes.
  • depth over throughput — the best few conversions, followed up at 6 and 12 months, matter more than the headcount.
  • the playbook ships open-source, so any community can run the funnel without us.
